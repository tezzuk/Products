# PART 1 — THE COLD CALL
*Goal: Hook them. Book the Google Meet. Nothing else.*
*Duration: 2–3 minutes max. Do NOT pitch the product on this call.*

---

## THE SCRIPT

"Hi, is this the owner of Campus E Shop?

Hi — my name is Aayushman. I'm a student at IIT Kanpur — I actually
shop at your store regularly.

I'm currently working on a research project on retail sales optimization
for small and mid-size stores — specifically looking at how stores like
yours can use their own sales data to improve inventory and revenue.

As part of the research, I've done a preliminary analysis of Campus E Shop
using publicly available data — reviews, foot traffic patterns, product
feedback. I found a few things that I think you'd find genuinely interesting.

I'd love to share the findings with you — it's a 15-minute video call,
completely free, no strings attached. It's part of the research and you'd
be getting a free analysis of your own store out of it.

Would you have 15 minutes sometime this week?"

"Mostly around peak hours, which product categories seem to be
underperforming based on customer reviews, and some patterns in
foot traffic through the week. The kind of things that are hard to
see when you're running the store day to day but show up clearly in the data.

I'd rather just show you — it's chart-based and makes much more sense
visually than over the phone. Takes 15 minutes."

The 15-minute call is just to share what I already found."

# PART 2 — THE GOOGLE MEET
*Goal: Show the pitch deck. Get them to share 2 weeks of data.*
*Duration: 15–20 minutes.*

---

## OPENING (first 60 seconds)

"Thanks for making the time. Quick context — I'm in my [year] year at IITK,
working on a research project around retail sales optimization. Campus E Shop
is one of the stores I picked because it's one I actually use, and because
the public data showed some really interesting patterns worth looking into.

Everything I'm about to show you is based on your Google Maps data,
Magicpin reviews, and customer feedback — no billing data at all yet.

Before I share my screen, three quick questions to make sure what I show
you is actually relevant to how you run things."

---

## THE QUESTIONS

**Q1 — The awareness question**
"On a typical week — which day brings in the most revenue for you?"

> They'll say Saturday or Sunday. Follow up:
> "And which hour on that day would you say is the peak?"
> Most won't know the exact hour. That uncertainty is your opening.

---

**Q2 — The dead stock question**
"If I asked you right now — which 3 products haven't sold well
in the last month — could you tell me off the top of your head?"

> They'll either hesitate or name something they *feel* is slow.
> Either answer works. Just nod and move on.

---

**Q3 — The cost question**
"Roughly — what do you think it costs you when you're overstocked
on a slow item for a full month? Just a ballpark."

> Most owners have never calculated this. Let them sit with it for
> a second. Don't fill the silence. Then say:
> "That's exactly what I want to show you."

---

## THE BRIDGE INTO THE DECK

"Based on those questions — and what I found in your public data —
I put together a quick analysis. Let me share my screen."

Completely free. If what you see is useful, we talk about the full
